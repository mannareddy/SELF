How It Happens

As the Sun fuses hydrogen into helium, its core slowly compresses and heats — raising the Sun’s output by roughly 10% every billion years. Within 1 to 1.5 billion years, that extra energy pushes Earth’s climate past the point of no return: a runaway greenhouse effect. The oceans evaporate, and since water vapor is itself a potent greenhouse gas, the warming accelerates its own loop. High in the atmosphere, solar ultraviolet radiation splits that water vapor into hydrogen and oxygen — the light hydrogen escapes to space, permanently drying out the planet. Long before the Sun ever becomes a Red Giant, Earth will already be uninhabitable.
